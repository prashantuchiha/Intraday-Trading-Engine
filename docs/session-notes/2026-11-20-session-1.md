## Design discussion

**DQ1 — flow from price update to position.**
- First answer: described market mechanics (supply/demand, matching) and asked how such a large system handles millions of trades.
- Feedback: our system is not the exchange. The exchange matches orders, the broker relays a feed, and we watch about 50 symbols. The system sees data, not reasons.
- Second answer: ML models decide buy/sell → order → a separate monitor manages exits.
- Feedback: the entry/exit split is good. There is no ML in the milestone. The missing steps are risk check, paper execution and persistence.

**DQ2 — checks before a paper buy, and who sets the fill price.**
- (a) Answer: check prices and which stocks are most likely to profit, because capital is fixed.
- Feedback: that is ranking/allocation (a strategy or portfolio concern), not a risk check. Risk checks are deterministic yes/no rules: entries enabled, data fresh, inside the allowed time window, enough **available** cash after reservations, per-trade and total exposure limits, position count, daily loss limit.
- (b) Answer: the broker at the stock market.
- Feedback: in paper mode no real order exists. **Our paper execution simulator** decides the fill, and it should be conservative: a buy fills at the ask (not LTP), plus slippage, plus explicit costs.
